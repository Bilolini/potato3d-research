What one view can tell us about a potato

A single camera records color and depth from above the conveyor. We tested ways to fill in the hidden underside and checked how much the colored registration pin influences CoRe++, a model that turns that view into a 3D shape.

Could we improve the hidden underside?

Copying bumps from the visible side

We tried reflecting details from the top to the bottom of a smooth potato model. Only 7 of 12 development potatoes had enough usable data for the comparison. Tuning the reflection helped by about 0.10 mm there. On a separate set, error rose by 0.06 mm across 28 measurable potatoes out of 36. The uncertainty included zero, so we kept the smooth shape as our baseline.

Using the conveyor to set the height

A height adjustment guided by the detailed reference lowered development error by 1.76 mm. An adjustment estimated from the camera image alone lowered error by 0.26 mm across 24 measurable potatoes out of 36 in a separate test. The uncertainty included zero. An audit of 18 acquisition groups found no independent measurement of the support surface under each potato at capture.

Does pin color change the predicted shape?

How much the shape moved

On fresh test potatoes, the pin edit moved the predicted surface more than the same color change on ordinary peel in 25 of 29 cases. The average extra movement was 0.49 mm, with a 95% interval of 0.31 to 0.63 mm across recording groups. The average stayed positive at 0.40 mm against the strongest peel patch. We set a minimum of six recording groups for confirmation. With five groups, this result describes what we observed in this set.

Fresh-potato results

Whether the shape got closer

Against the detailed 3D reference, six development potatoes improved, three worsened and one tied. Average error fell by 0.13 mm. On 3R5-2 the shape moved 2.02 mm and error fell 0.43 mm. On R8-2 it moved 1.84 mm and error rose 0.22 mm.

Accuracy results
How we checked the comparison

We ran the released CoRe++ model on CPU and verified that the pin was visible to it. We applied identical color changes at the pin and at three peel patches, with depth held fixed. We matched the model's random settings across conditions over 16 repeats. Expanding the reconstruction grid removed 321 boundary contacts in 800 predictions, and the pin effect remained positive on the same 9 of 10 development potatoes.

Does the response follow the color?

We exchanged only the color pixels between the pin and a peel patch, then repeated the same color edit at each location. The depth readings stayed at their original positions.

Across ten development potatoes, the response shifted toward the transplanted pixels on 9 of 10. It fully switched in that direction for at least two of three tested locations on 8 of 10 potatoes.

One potato originally responded more to peel edits and switched in the opposite direction at all three locations. The exchanges left visible seams and sometimes a pin rim, so the results describe the effect of color in these edited images.

What is still open?

We still need to find out whether CoRe++ recognizes the pin itself and whether physically removing it changes reconstruction accuracy. For the earlier height question, we still lack an independent measurement of where the conveyor actually supports each potato.

This summary draws on archived experiments. The three held-out cohorts stayed closed.
